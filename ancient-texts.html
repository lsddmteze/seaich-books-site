Volume 1 · 371 pages

Introduction and Proem

What Is Mormonism? Apostasy, Orthodoxy, and the Eternal Gospel

The opening volume frames the whole series: what Mormonism claims about itself, what the “great apostasy” means in early-Christian terms, how orthodoxy formed — and the case for an eternal gospel underlying restoration claims. It sets the method the later volumes follow: ancient texts first, then the Latter-day Saint reading of them.

About this volume

Volume 2 · 628 pages

The Mormon Concept of Deity

Father, Son, Holy Spirit, and the Divine Council

The series’ doctrinal core: the Latter-day Saint understanding of God — Father, Son, and Holy Ghost as distinct beings — set against the ancient divine-council worldview of the Hebrew Bible (Deuteronomy 32, Psalm 82) and early Jewish and Christian sources. The volume traces how the council of gods in ancient texts illuminates the Mormon concept of deity.

About this volume

Volume 3 · 531 pages

The Premortal Life of the Soul

Pre-Existence and the Divine Nature of Man in the Bible, the Dead Sea Scrolls, Ancient Judaism, and Early Christianity

What human beings are and where they came from: the preexistence of spirits, the heavenly council’s role in the divine plan, and the ancient roots of the doctrine that the soul has a divine origin. Draws on biblical, pseudepigraphal, and early Christian witnesses.

About this volume

Volume 4 · 585 pages

Grace, Works, and Deification

Salvation, the Atonement, the Three Degrees of Glory, and Theosis in the Bible and the Early Church

The doctrine of salvation: grace and works, the ancient Christian doctrine of theosis (deification — humans becoming like God), and the three degrees of glory. The volume answers critics — including a direct response to assessments like Sterling McMurrin’s — with the ancient texts themselves as evidence.

About this volume

Volume 5 · 828 pages

The Temple

Ancient Temple Symbolism, the Jerusalem Temple, and the Modern Temple

Temple theology across the ancient world: the symbolism of the Jerusalem temple, its rituals and architecture, and their relationship to the modern Latter-day Saint temple. Builds on Seaich’s 1981 SEHA paper on cherubim and temple symbolism and his Gorgias Press volume A Great Mystery: The Secret of the Jerusalem Temple.

About this volume

Volume 6 · 485 pages

Gnosticism and the Book of Abraham

The Nag Hammadi Texts, the Papyri, and the Facsimiles

The closing volume takes on the hardest problems: Gnostic literature from Nag Hammadi, the Joseph Smith papyri, and the Book of Abraham facsimiles — testing Latter-day Saint claims against the ancient sources with the series’ full scholarly apparatus.

Questions this collection answers

Are Latter-day Saints Christian?

Seaich argues that core Latter-day Saint teachings match beliefs of the earliest Christians that later creeds set aside. His study reads Latter-day Saint doctrine beside the Bible, the Dead Sea Scrolls, the Nag Hammadi texts and the early Church Fathers, and presents Mormonism as a return to the oldest beliefs rather than a break from biblical Christianity.

Did ancient Israel worship a Heavenly Mother?

He reads the “Yahweh and his Asherah” inscriptions and the biblical Queen of Heaven as evidence that many Israelites did. Volume 2 covers the Godhead and the Trinity, El and Yahweh, the divine council of Psalm 82, and Asherah and the Heavenly Mother.

Did early Christians believe we lived before birth?

He traces pre-existence from Jeremiah 1:5 to Origen and explains why the later Church abandoned it. Volume 3 covers the premortal life of the soul, from Genesis and Jeremiah to Enoch, the Dead Sea Scrolls, Paul, John and Origen — ancient witnesses for a belief the earliest Christians held.

Do Latter-day Saints teach salvation by works?

He separates salvation, a gift of grace, from reward according to works, a distinction he finds in both Paul and James. Volume 4 covers salvation and reward, Paul and James, exaltation, the three degrees of glory and theosis in the Church Fathers, showing grace and works as two different things, not rivals.

Where did the temple endowment come from?

He compares the rites with Solomon’s Temple, early Christian ritual and Freemasonry, and argues they restore an older temple religion. Volume 5 covers the endowment, eternal marriage, baptism for the dead, Solomon’s Temple and a full study of Mormonism and Freemasonry.

Can the Book of Abraham facsimiles be read in Egyptian terms?

He argues that Joseph Smith’s explanations agree in key points with Egyptian ideas of sacrifice, enthronement and rebirth, even where they differ from modern Egyptology. Volume 6 covers the Nag Hammadi library, the Gospel of Philip, the Joseph Smith Papyri and the three facsimiles.
