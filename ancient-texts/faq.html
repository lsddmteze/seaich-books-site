Ancient Texts and Mormonism · FAQ

Questions about the series

Short, factual answers about the six volumes, their sources and when they appear.

Updated November 3, 2026

What is Ancient Texts and Mormonism?

A six-volume study by Eugene Seaich (1925–2006) of ancient texts and Latter-day Saint theology, subtitled “A Historical and Theological Study of Primitive Christianity, Early Apocrypha, Patristic Writings, and Restored Latter-day Saint Doctrine.” The 2026 Annotated Edition is revised and edited, with a foreword, by his grandson Eric Hendrickson.

Which ancient texts does it use?

The Hebrew Bible in its oldest recoverable form, the pseudepigrapha, the Nag Hammadi library and the early Christian fathers, read alongside Latter-day Saint theology.

What do the six volumes cover?

Volume 1 — Introduction and Proem (What Is Mormonism? Apostasy, Orthodoxy, and the Eternal Gospel); Volume 2 — The Mormon Concept of Deity (Father, Son, Holy Spirit, and the Divine Council); Volume 3 — The Premortal Life of the Soul (The Premortal Life of the Soul); Volume 4 — Grace, Works, and Deification (Grace, Works, and Deification); Volume 5 — The Temple (Ancient Temple Symbolism, the Jerusalem Temple, and the Modern Temple); Volume 6 — Gnosticism and the Book of Abraham (The Nag Hammadi Texts, the Papyri, and the Facsimiles).

How long is the series?

The six print volumes run 371, 628, 531, 585, 828 and 485 pages: 3,428 pages in all.

In what formats will it appear?

Six paperback volumes in print, and four ebook editions: Parts I, II and III, and an omnibus. The omnibus Kindle ebook — all six volumes in one ebook — is live on Amazon.

When will it be available?

The omnibus Kindle ebook is live on Amazon now. The publication schedule and prices for the other editions are announced as each volume clears proof. Sign up for series news to hear first.

Is it an official publication of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints?

No. It is independent scholarship. Seaich’s comparisons of Latter-day Saint teaching with ancient sources are his interpretation, not official Church teaching.

How does it relate to Understanding Mormonism?

Both are by Eugene Seaich. Understanding Mormonism is a single volume on grace, works, salvation and exaltation; Ancient Texts and Mormonism is his six-volume study, in which Volume 4 treats salvation, theosis and the three degrees of glory.

Independent scholarship; not an official publication of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints.
