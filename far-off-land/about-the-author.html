A life across disciplines

Seaich studied at the University of Utah, where he earned five degrees, including a Master of Fine Arts in 1952 and a Ph.D. in Music in 1958. He spent 1955–56 in Germany as a Fulbright grantee, affiliated as a student with the University of Munich. He worked as a registered pharmacist and wrote on ancient religious texts.

In the late 1950s he turned to consciousness research. The Far-Off Land, written in 1959, was his attempt to join first-person observation of the drug experience with what he knew of mythology, mysticism, literature and the psychology of memory. Later in life he wrote on ancient religious texts and their parallels.

In his own words: how the book began

In his unpublished autobiography, Seaich remembered the late 1950s as a brief, open season. He and five university colleagues each put in ten dollars and ordered LSD by mail from K&K Laboratories in New York. The university’s chemistry and pharmacy departments helped divide and prepare it; Sandoz, the manufacturer, sent an envelope of instructions; and Dr. Louis Goodman, co-author of the leading pharmacology text, added his own advice. Looking back, Seaich wrote:

“Today, any of these individuals or institutions would have immediately called for the police and had us arrested!”

The editor: Eric Hendrickson

Eric Hendrickson is Seaich’s grandson and the publisher of Seaich Legacy Publications. At twenty-two he was handed a copy of his grandfather’s unpublished autobiography and learned of his psychedelic research for the first time. After Seaich died in 2006, he found the hand-typed manuscript of The Far-Off Land in his grandfather’s library. Bringing it to print meant working past family reluctance about the subject. The 2026 Complete Annotated Edition presents the text in the restored edition and adds commentary connecting it to current research.
