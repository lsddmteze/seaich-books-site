The book in three minutes

What it is. A philosophical essay in eleven chapters and a field journal of nine dated sessions with LSD and mescaline, written by Eugene Seaich in 1959–60, when the research was legal.

What it argues. That the drugs do not invent visions but open the oldest layers of memory: childhood, myth, the “far-off land” behind everyday consciousness.

Why read it now. Seaich described ego dissolution, the role of music and the value of difficult experiences decades before research measured them.

What this edition adds. The text in the restored edition, commentary linking it to current research, notes, a glossary and facsimile pages.

What it isn’t. A guide to using psychedelics, or medical advice.
