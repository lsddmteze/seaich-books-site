The Complete Annotated Edition

Inside the book

In 1959, the Salt Lake City scholar John Eugene Seaich wrote The Far-Off Land, a philosophical essay and field journal on LSD, mescaline, memory and consciousness. This Complete Annotated Edition presents his text in the restored edition, with the nine experiment journals (August 1959 – October 1960) and commentary connecting his observations to today's research.

The Buried World

Memory, Consciousness, and Primal Experience

Why the drugs seemed to open the deepest, earliest layers of memory, and what that suggests about the mind.

Childhood, Lost Paradise, and Innocence

Childhood as our first vision of the world, and the myths of a lost paradise found in every culture.

The Sacred Intoxicant

Global History of Altered States

Sacred intoxicants from Soma and the Eleusinian mysteries to opium and hashish, and why people have always sought them.

Chaos and Creation

Archetypes and Pre-Rational Being

Archetypal images, the pre-rational mind, and the line between visionary experience and breakdown.

V

The Naked Religion

Matter, Spirit, and Sacred Phenomenology

Nature encountered as a religious experience, with D. H. Lawrence’s “vast old religion” of New Mexico.

VI

First Contact

Phenomenology of Psychedelic Perception

What the first hours feel like: familiar objects taking on new meaning and the world seen as if for the first time.

VII

The Inner Light

Luminescence, Radiance, and the Neuroscience of Light

Light as the oldest symbol of consciousness, and the radiance that fills the visions.

VIII

The Mirror of Self

Ego Dissolution and Existential Anxiety

Doubt, the loss of meaning, and the “absolute nimbo of zero-nothingness” at the bottom of the psyche.

IX

The Dark Night & New Dawn

Nirvana, Despair, and Liberation

Passing through emptiness to renewal, with Zen, Mozart and the return of joy.

X

Beyond Huxley

Scientific and Anthropological Implications

Where Seaich parts company with Aldous Huxley, and what psychology and anthropology could learn from the drug experience.

XI

The Perennial Religion

Spiritual and Existential Restoration

The poetic vision as a way back to the sacred, closing with Aeschylus on heaven and earth.

Part Two · The experiment journals

Nine sessions, August 1959 – October 1960

Timed entries written during each session, with the author’s later notes.

All nine sessions

Session 1 of 9 · August 1959

The first session

Mescaline

The opening entry runs from mid-morning into the night. Nausea gives way to color, then to music: he notes a long stretch of “sheer bliss with music (Wagner)” before visitors break the spell.

Journal · August 1959
8:00City lights (in darkness) look like jewels. Very intense and colorful.

Session 2 of 9 · November 8, 1959

The threshold

LSD

A deliberately small first trial of LSD. At first he believes the drug is not working; by mid-afternoon he records music playing in his mind “much more palpable than usual daydreaming.”

Journal · November 8, 1959
4:30Poetry (T. S. Eliot, Four Quartets) seems to have much more intense meaning.

Session 3 of 9 · November 21, 1959

Testing an antidote

LSD

Seaich tests whether a vitamin, nicotinamide, can blunt the effects. It barely does. A walk at dusk ties the experience to childhood memory, the book’s central theme.

Journal · November 21, 1959
5:30Went for a walk in the dusk. The winter scene had a novel freshness about it. Seemed to be pervaded by a long-forgotten childhood sensation.

Session 4 of 9 · December 17, 1959

The first full mystical experience

LSD

The longest and most intense of the early sessions: kaleidoscopic visions, Mahler’s Third Symphony, and an evening by the Christmas-tree lights in which he recreated “the sensation of Christmas as I knew it in childhood.”

Journal · December 17, 1959
3:45Can hear crickets chirping — am back in a childhood scene — a summer evening — this most unusual!

Session 5 of 9 · January 3, 1960

A challenging experience, and recovery

LSD

Bliss with Mozart turns into emptiness and “utter zero-ness.” Then the mood lifts again. Seaich records the difficult hours as plainly as the good ones.

Journal · January 3, 1960
2:40World seems cold, strange, empty. Can’t make my inner self work this time.

Session 6 of 9 · January 23, 1960

The great childhood-memory session

Mescaline

After an hour of regret and nausea come color, flame-like visions and a sense of “creative energy, revitalization.” He ends the day in “a harmonious emotion of outpouring goodwill and happiness.”

Journal · January 23, 1960
1:30All splendid fire and heat; a divine force pulsating in vision beyond all description.

Session 7 of 9 · February 28, 1960

A threshold-state experiment

LSD

A small-dose experiment in mood. Listening to Tristan, he finds childhood recollections “trying to draw me back against my will into their sinister magic.”

Journal · February 28, 1960
5:00A strange world of otherness, neither euphoric nor dysphoric, ensued.

Session 8 of 9 · March 5, 1960

A lesson in set and setting

LSD

Telephone calls and interruptions cut across the peak of the session, and he records what was lost. The entry reads today as an early account of why setting matters.

Journal · March 5, 1960
4:00Light! Pure light! Just to sit and stare into translucent Being!

Session 9 of 9 · October 22, 1960

The closing entry

LSD

The last journal, written after a seven-month pause. It closes the record of the experiments.

Journal · October 22, 1960
3:05Suddenly noticed a beauty in the sky which I had long forgotten. A cloud formation recalled some distant notion of adventure.
