One line

A 1959 philosophical essay and field journal by the scholar John Eugene Seaich: nine dated self-experiments with LSD and mescaline, in the restored edition and annotated with modern research.

Short description (≈60 words)

In 1959, the Salt Lake City scholar John Eugene Seaich wrote The Far-Off Land, a philosophical essay and field journal on LSD, mescaline, memory and consciousness. This Complete Annotated Edition presents his text in the restored edition, with the nine experiment journals (August 1959 – October 1960) and commentary connecting his observations to today's research.

Long description (≈150 words)

In 1959, when LSD and mescaline were still legal and studied openly, John Eugene Seaich, a Salt Lake City musician, pharmacist and scholar of ancient religious texts, began a series of carefully dated self-experiments and wrote a philosophical essay about what they revealed. The Far-Off Land argues that these substances open the deepest, earliest layers of memory, the “far-off land” of childhood and the unconscious, and traces that idea through myth, mysticism, music and the history of sacred intoxicants. Kept in a family library for decades, the typescript was recovered by Seaich’s grandson. This Complete Annotated Edition restores the text from the 1959 original and adds the nine experiment journals (August 1959 – October 1960), commentary linking Seaich’s observations to research on the default mode network, ego dissolution and mystical experience, notes, a glossary, a bibliography, a timeline and facsimile pages from the typescript.
